# Deliverable 1: User Interviews & Problem Statement
**Course Project 2: Design a Digital Wellness App**  
**Project Name:** Later Lane — Focus & Urge Parking  
**Prototype URL:** [https://later-lane.vercel.app](https://later-lane.vercel.app)

---

## 1. User Research Methodology

To uncover the underlying drivers of digital distraction among students and knowledge workers, we conducted semi-structured qualitative interviews with **four potential users** representing different study domains and daily habits. 

All interviews were conducted prior to introducing the Later Lane prototype to avoid confirmation bias and to understand their native coping mechanisms, existing tools, and emotional reactions to distraction.

---

## 2. Anonymized User Interview Summaries

### Participant A (CS Undergrad, 21 — "The Multitasker")
* **Current Study Routine:** 4–6 hours of coding and problem sets daily, split between laptop and secondary monitor.
* **Current Tools Used:** Forest, Apple Screen Time, Chrome tab groups.
* **Identified Difficulties:**
  > *"When my code throws a compiler error or I get stuck on an algorithm for more than two minutes, my thumb reaches for my phone like muscle memory. I unlock it and find myself on Instagram reels before I even realize I decided to check it."*
* **Existing Alternatives & Points of Failure:**
  - *Forest:* *"When Forest kills my tree because I needed to check a 2-factor authentication code or an urgent message, I get annoyed, give up, and abandon the session completely."*
  - *Screen Time limits:* *"I just tap 'Ignore limit for 15 minutes'. It feels like a nagging parent that I constantly disobey, which creates lingering guilt."*
* **Key Need:** An immediate outlet to capture sudden impulses without abandoning or aborting the focus session.

---

### Participant B (Biology Pre-Med, 20 — "The Anxious Memorizer")
* **Current Study Routine:** High-volume reading, textbook chapter summaries, and flashcards.
* **Current Tools Used:** Pomodoro timer (standard 25/5 iOS clock app), physical notebook.
* **Identified Difficulties:**
  > *"Reading 40 pages of cell biology gets monotonous. Whenever a difficult pathway comes up, I feel a wave of anxiety that I won't finish in time, so I check my phone to get a quick dopamine boost and avoid the discomfort."*
* **Existing Alternatives & Points of Failure:**
  - *Standard Pomodoro:* *"The 5-minute break is my danger zone. I open TikTok or YouTube during the break, lose track of time, and 5 minutes turns into 45 minutes."*
* **Key Need:** Understanding *why* the urge happened (boredom vs. task confusion vs. anxiety) and having a clear guardrail on breaks.

---

### Participant C (Humanities & Design Major, 22 — "The Creative Procrastinator")
* **Current Tools Used:** Notion, Do Not Disturb, Pen & paper.
* **Identified Difficulties:**
  > *"Random thoughts constantly pop into my head while writing essays — 'Did I reply to that email?', 'Check ticket prices', 'Look up a design reference'. If I don't act immediately, I fear I'll forget it, so I switch tabs and end up down a Wikipedia rabbit hole."*
* **Existing Alternatives & Points of Failure:**
  - *Pen and Paper:* *"I write thoughts on sticky notes, but they clutter my desk, and I never look at them again after the session."*
* **Key Need:** A structured digital shelf to park thoughts and review whether they actually matter once the work block ends.

---

### Participant D (Economics Student, 23 — "The Chronic Notification Checker")
* **Current Tools Used:** WhatsApp Web, Discord, Slack, Mac Do Not Disturb.
* **Identified Difficulties:**
  > *"Group chats and notifications vibrate constantly. Even on Do Not Disturb, I have 'phantom vibration syndrome' — wondering if someone texted me about the group project."*
* **Existing Alternatives & Points of Failure:**
  - *All-or-nothing blockers:* *"Turning off my phone completely makes me anxious that I'll miss an emergency family call or an announcement from my professor."*
* **Key Need:** A clear triage method for notifications (VIP vs. batching) combined with peace of mind.

## 3. Summary of Existing Alternatives & Market Gaps

| Tool / Solution | Approach | Critical Gap & User Failure Mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Forest / Plantie** | Gamified guilt & punitive trees dying | Punishes accidental exits; causes frustration and total abandonment when real-life interruptions occur. |
| **Apple Screen Time / Digital Wellbeing** | Hard application limits & lockouts | Trivially bypassed via "Ignore limit"; induces moral guilt without addressing *why* the user reached for the phone. |
| **Standard Pomodoro Timers** | Rigid 25m on / 5m off cycles | Zero support for impulse parking; 5-minute breaks routinely morph into 40-minute doomscrolling sessions. |
| **Physical Scrap Paper** | Scribbling thoughts on notes | Lacks longitudinal reflection, pattern analytics, or expiry review to prove impulses fade on their own. |

---

## 4. Problem Statement

> **Target User:** University students and knowledge workers engaged in self-directed deep work.  
> **Core Difficulty:** When facing cognitive friction (confusing steps, boredom, or fatigue), users reflexively turn to their smartphones for instant stimulation. Existing tools use punitive, all-or-nothing restrictions that produce guilt and friction, while offering zero support for the emotional triggers behind the impulse or the tendency for short study breaks to devolve into prolonged doomscrolling.  
> **Desired Improvement:** A digital wellness focus companion that validates urges rather than shaming them — allowing users to "park" distracting impulses in under 5 seconds, surface empathetic interventions for task friction, audit notification clutter, guard break boundaries, and realize through non-judgmental pattern reflection that over 70% of phone impulses naturally fade if simply paused.
